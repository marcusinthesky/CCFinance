\section{Corpus lineage and source pilot}

The historical publisher list selects English-language Fine News records
using the hostname in metadata, with the URL as fallback, and includes
dot-delimited subdomains. The frozen input contains 14,807,287 distinct
IDs across 168 shards. The replication package records the upstream and
staging revisions. A fresh upstream selection still requires row-level
comparison with these frozen IDs and bodies.

The retrospective pilot expands sampled rows within each of four pinned
release frames (Table~\ref{tab:app-source}). A cluster draw selects a
file and then a row group.
The product of those two probabilities determines the inverse-probability
expansion of its counts. The ratio of the resulting Hansen--Hurwitz
totals gives weighted publisher yield, with exploratory 95\% linearized
$t$ intervals on three degrees of freedom. The manifests preserve the
release versions, draw probabilities and observed counts. Each frame
has its own upstream filtering and deduplication, so the comparison
concerns physical rows. Estimating unique stories, overlap or financial
coverage outside the registry would require separate sampling.

The Fine News matched-total interval is 2.15--18.67 million physical
records, encompassing the 14.81 million staged IDs.
The pinned release reports 219,192,987 English records. Staged IDs amount to
6.76\% of that count, pending an independent replay of the selection.
Four draws offer limited precision for a population total.
The Fine News, Infini-News, RefinedWeb and FineWeb-HQ frames contain
168, 49,748, 5,534 and 9,246 files respectively. The four selected
Fine News files contain 437, 519, 71 and 319 row groups. Each sampled

\section{Extractor and metadata audits}

\paragraph{Exact company spans.}
The microbenchmark takes the 50 lowest seeded ID hashes per
publication year from 2019 through 2023 among consistently dated,
canonical English articles in the staging snapshot. A lead contains the
first 150 whitespace-delimited words or the entire shorter body. A
GPT-6.2-sol medium primary pass covered 250 leads, including the
40-lead pilot. A separate GPT-6.2-sol low pass covered the first 12
ranks in each of the five years. The passes agreed exactly on 52 of
60 repeated leads
(span F1 95.68\%). Disagreements were resolved against the guide
before scoring. The repeat annotator had access to published pilot
examples. The resulting model reference contains 712 spans across 250
leads, 73 with none. The guide includes named businesses
and excludes products, bare tickers and nonbusiness organizations.

Stanza 1.11.0 uses the English \texttt{ontonotes\_charlm} model with
combined tokenization and multiword-token expansion. GLiNER2.5 Small
uses the deployed company schema and cutoff 0.5 \citep{stanza,gliner25}.
Scoring matches Stanza ORG or GLiNER company spans by article ID and
exact half-open Unicode offsets. A correctly recognized noncompany ORG
therefore counts as a false positive for this task.
Table~\ref{tab:app-spans} reports a GLiNER advantage of 10.34
micro-F1 percentage points on this company-specific task, with a
paired 95\% interval of $[5.84,14.56]$ over 2,000 passage bootstraps
within publication year. Differences use unrounded scores. When a
prediction and reference need only share a character, maximum one-to-one
matching gives F1 of 58.13\% for Stanza and 58.54\% for GLiNER.
The paired difference is 0.41 points ($-3.36$ to $+4.40$) over 2,000
year-stratified resamples. Both evaluations retain the business-only reference.
Stanford's published 88.8\% OntoNotes and 92.1\% CoNLL03 scores are
all-class micro-F1 on their benchmark splits \citep{stanza}. In the
local task, a possessive attached to the Credit Suisse Group AG span
cost Stanza its pair with UBS Group AG, while GLiNER missed BofA in a
lead with five stocks and lost five of 15 possible pairs. These are
mention pairs before dated issuer resolution.

\paragraph{Article, mention and window metadata.}
GLiNER2.5 Small processes 384-word windows with a 64-word overlap.
Each window receives a softmax distribution over seven article forms
and an independent score for each of six event families. The article
inherits the form predicted with the highest confidence across its
windows. Event families are evaluated per window at a cutoff of 0.5,
so a window can have several families or none. The extractor returns
body offsets for company, person, public-body and place spans. Each
company span receives separate softmax scores for local relevance and
investor-facing sentiment. Within an article--issuer pair, the linker
takes the largest substantive-relevance score and averages the sentiment
vectors using mention link scores as weights. The mention-level audit below
evaluates local labels before that issuer-level aggregation.

The seed-46 confirmation excludes seed-45 development articles and
samples 400 units from each usable-corpus frame. For form and sentiment,
half the allocation follows predicted class size and half balances
classes subject to availability. Events split equally between windows
with all scores below 0.5 and positive windows stratified by top family.
The GPT-6 Luna low-effort judge sees the article, marked mention in
local context or event window, with predictions withheld. A second Luna
call and a GPT-6.1 Terra low-effort call independently label the same
100 random units per frame. Estimates use inverse-inclusion weights
and article-cluster multiplier intervals holding stratum totals fixed
(Table~\ref{tab:app-metadata}).

The press-release discrepancy persists using usable article--issuer links
($-37.3$ points, 95\% interval $[-47.0,-27.6]$). Counting three
insufficient-context articles as releases would raise the weighted
reference prevalence from 4.18\% to 4.73\%. Sentiment labels have opposite
positive and negative polarity on 7.4\% of weighted extracted mentions.
Weighted sentiment agreement is 82.3\% on the Luna repeat and 57.2\%
between Luna and Terra. The primary judge found at least one event
family in 160 of 200 sampled windows with all predictions below threshold.
The separate 10,000-article extraction benchmark ran at 106.1 articles
per second with 7.22 GiB peak reserved CUDA memory. Synthetic examples
guided development and contribute zero weight to confirmation estimates.

\paragraph{Sentence and company sentiment.}
The primary judge assigns positive, negative or neutral sentiment to
358 of 400 marked mentions. We compare GLiNER's company label with
the root sentiment of the containing sentence from Stanford CoreNLP
4.5.10, collapsing the two intensities in each direction. Mixed and
unassessable predictions miss the judged class. One Stanford sentence
exceeds the 200-token parsing limit. Weighted three-class macro-F1 is
46.79\% for GLiNER and 40.84\% for Stanford, a paired difference of
5.95 points [$-1.33$, $+13.98$]. Polarity reversals occur on 8.38\%
and 10.73\% of directionally judged units, respectively. A fresh
GPT-6.1 Terra pass agreed with the primary judge on 10 of 40 blinded
cases. It marked 23 targets as invalid company spans, including a
product and a political party. Both span validity and the financial
interpretation of the surrounding text affect this comparison.

\section{Issuer identity, focus and coverage}

The seed-43 confirmation samples extracted spans by alias frequency
and the number of issuers sharing a key, and draws a separate uniform
sample of articles containing at least one extracted company span.
Article judges receive up to 12,000 body characters. Both draws are
independent of linker predictions. Model
judges receive marked text and neutral issuer search with the candidate
link withheld. A correct link names the judged issuer or the same listed
security on the article date. Weighted intervals are linearized. Unlinked
precision is the share of abstentions judged to have no eligible issuer
(Table~\ref{tab:app-links}).
Cohen's $\kappa$ is 0.935 for the repeated judge on 164 mentions and
0.881 for the independent audit judge on 339.

\paragraph{Selecting a subject rule.}
We compared mention counts, the position of an issuer's first mention
as a fraction of body length, and its highest substantive-relevance
score. The full grid crossed six count cutoffs, four position cutoffs
and 19 relevance cutoffs. Three mentions, with no further restriction,
gave the highest development F1. On held-out articles, counting two
mentions recovered more subjects and gave a higher F1 than counting
three (Table~\ref{tab:app-focus-rules}). Seven development and five
held-out bodies exceeded
the judge's 12,000-character limit. In one Schlumberger earnings-call
transcript, the third linked mentions of Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and
Bank of America all occurred after character 20,000. The full-body
rule selected all three banks, whereas the judge identified Schlumberger
as the subject from the visible prefix.

effective date, while the prior window controls when frequency evidence
could enter the decision. Explicit anchors can resolve a mention directly.

with a separate operating score of 0.5. Ambiguous mentions retain their
candidate lists. Frequency strata use fewer than ten, ten through 999,
and at least 1,000 occurrences, crossed with zero, one or multiple
matching issuers.

\section{Body embeddings and issuer conditioning}

The production EmbeddingGemma artifact \citep{embeddinggemma2025} stores
768 FP16 coordinates per body with a 2,048-token limit. The census
reconciled all IDs, positions and text flags and checked finite values
and norms. All 14,807,287 frozen bodies contained text. A blank-body
fixture exercises the zero-vector convention. In the overnight panel,
the production tokenizer truncated 44 of 996 bodies after adding the
document prefix and special tokens. For six of 1,104 article--issuer
rows, the third linked mention ended beyond the visible text. The focus
screen uses mentions from the full captured body.

In the separate issuer-conditioning pilot, a story is eligible when
two companies have distinguishable accounts under the model reference.
A paired success requires both company-conditioned views to prefer
their own name-masked account. Luna identified 18 eligible stories
among 150 held out, of which 16 fit every encoder. Terra found eight
eligible stories in the audit. The primary and audit denominators fell
below their planned minima of 60 and 20. The observed gains from
selecting issuer sentences motivate confirmation on a larger sample
(Table~\ref{tab:app-vectors}).

where the companies share their sentence mask. OpenSearch mention
pooling scored 4/24. The production vectors support the fixed-encoder
exercise while these issuer-specific experiments remain exploratory.

\paragraph{Equity market volatility.}
The July 2025 manuscript and archive v1 supply the reference tracker
and specifications \citep{baker2025ms,baker2025archive}. Six archive
reruns recover the monthly fits at displayed precision. The substituted
tracker uses dictionary-hit shares in 196 publisher-month cells.
Divide each publisher's shares by its dispersion over the 40
calibration months through December 2022, average across publishers
and rescale to the calibration mean VIX. The comparative fit uses an
intercept and a contemporaneous tracker against each common outcome.
For each of 10,000 bootstrap samples, we draw six-month circular
blocks with replacement until we have 49 observations, trimming the
last block. Both trackers and the common outcome use the same months
in each draw. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resulting
differences in squared correlations give the paired interval.
Realized-volatility $R^2$ is 0.336 for CCFinance and 0.569 for
the archived tracker, a gap of $-0.233$ $[-0.311,+0.041]$.

Table~\ref{tab:app-emv} drops March and April 2020 first from the two
fits, then also from the calibration of publisher dispersions and the
VIX scale. With all four publishers, the 47-month VIX gap rounds to
$-0.041$ under both calibrations. Omitting Forbes gives the largest
shortfalls among the four publisher omissions in every specification.
Using only the \emph{Wall Street Journal} gives 49-month $R^2$ values
of 0.110 for VIX and 0.120 for realized volatility. The archive's
monthly benchmark supplies the eleven-paper composite, so the
correlation of 0.397 compares the single-publisher tracker with that
composite. Requiring 15 publication dates drops three sparse cells and
yields a full-calendar VIX gap of $-0.142$. Ending calibration in
December 2021 gives a gap of $-0.167$.

\paragraph{Overnight portfolios.}
The fixed panel operationalizes a selected design from arXiv v6 and
reference archive v2 \citep{lopez2025v6,lopez2026archive}, using captured
bodies and frozen Yahoo chart prices in place of proprietary headlines
and CRSP. The clock parser recovered 11,196 first-publication markers.
Of those, 2,738 fell outside an overnight interval and 274 were captured
after cutoff. Among the remaining 8,184 articles, 5,392 had no fixed-roster
link. The focus rule rejected 2,510 on-roster links. Of the 995 links
remaining, five failed a within-session similarity screen, one repeated
a recent title and one failed the price-movement headline screen. The
988 survivors passed dated listing, vector and price checks and add
943 unique bodies and 667 firm-sessions. The same 20 of 32 rolling
windows fit, though the recovered panel changes training and evaluation
rows. Allowing LBFGS up to 1,000 iterations eliminated all 16 baseline
drift warnings. At the original inverse regularization strength $C=1$,
nine of 738 chosen security--session signals changed, leaving 39 both-leg
sessions and mean drift of $-8.73$ bp. Stronger regularization
($C=0.1$) changed 221 signals, leaving six both-leg sessions and
mean drift of $-13.67$ bp. Both refits evaluate the original 277 days.

\section{Access and reproduction}

Common Crawl makes its captures available and preserves third-party
rights \citep{commoncrawlterms}. Infini-News documents CC BY 4.0 for
derived metadata and an academic research use condition for bulk text
\citep{lazzaroni2026}. Fine News identifies AGPL-3.0-only for its code
and assigns no common license to underlying stories \citep{solovev2026}.
The CCFinance selector reads Fine News Parquet outputs through DuckDB.
The project code uses Apache-2.0. The Baker
and Lopez-Lira--Tang archives are listed under CC BY 4.0
\citep{baker2025archive,lopez2026archive}. Proprietary inputs such as
Factiva, RavenPack and CRSP have separate access requirements.

Exact reconstruction requires the frozen inputs. During review the
project code and artifact store are private. Anonymous checks on
22 November 2026 reached the public upstream release and encountered
authentication on the staging snapshot. We plan a post-review release
of code and derived records with artifact-specific terms. The historical
publisher registry, capture identifiers, copy decisions, mention offsets
and issuer mappings can be documented independently of the article bodies.
Vector redistribution requires a separate terms review.
Frozen labels permit rescoring, while fresh adjudication requires access
to the corresponding models. Codex assisted with manuscript preparation.
