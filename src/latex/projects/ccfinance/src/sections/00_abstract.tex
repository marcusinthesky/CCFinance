A financial news study can be reconstructed when the observed text,
issuer and eligibility rule behind each observation are recoverable.
We construct CCFinance from a frozen selection of Common Crawl News
and trace archived article versions through publication-date checks,
deduplication and dated issuer linking. Among 100 reference firms,
94 have at least 47 usable linked articles in each year from 2018
through 2022, and 57 meet the same threshold when counted articles
must also satisfy a company-focus screen. Model-adjudicated audits measure the
change in subject precision and recall that accompanies this loss of
coverage. Three financial applications show how the evidence changes
with the observation. A publisher-month volatility index supports a
common-calendar tracking comparison, bank co-mentions reveal sensitivity
to publisher mix, and additional publication clocks expand the number
of sessions on which both legs of an overnight return strategy can
be evaluated. Public archives supply source material, while exact
reconstruction of the reported samples requires access to the frozen
inputs and derived artifacts.
