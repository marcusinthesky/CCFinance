\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A financial news corpus becomes useful for an empirical design when
the researcher can state which version of a story entered the
analysis, why it was assigned to an issuer and when that version
could have been observed. The relevant accounting follows the study
unit. A publisher-month index needs evidence about the denominator of
captured stories, while a bank network also needs to establish which
institutions those stories name and how many copies contribute to an
edge. A firm-session portfolio adds a cutoff and needs enough
eligible securities for its legs. Because each restriction changes
the sample, an eligibility ledger belongs alongside the reported
estimate.

CCFinance records these steps from a frozen source selection and
provides audits of identity, focus and supporting metadata. The
upstream web archives and their documentation are publicly
accessible under their respective terms. Access to the project
repository and frozen processed artifacts is restricted, and
anonymous access to the exact staging snapshot remains unverified.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app-access} records acquisition routes,
requirements and rights by artifact. The recipes can regenerate the
reported analysis when those inputs are restored. For a new study,
the same record of selection decisions can show whether tightening
the sample improves the intended measurement at a cost in firms,
dates or portfolio support.
