# Writing the CCFinance paper

Begin with the object whose properties matter to the financial question. A crawl
record gives evidence about a page at capture. An overnight firm return requires
evidence about a particular issuer and what was available before a trading
cutoff. A reader who understands either object still needs the steps that connect
them. Introduce a new unit, timing convention or term where the reader first
needs it.

Use a longer sentence when a definition, condition or qualification belongs to
the same inference. Give the main clause an identifiable subject and verb.
Technical nouns such as *coverage* and *exposure* earn their place through
consistent definitions. Choose the verb with equal care. A timestamp *records*
a capture, a rule *defines* eligibility, and a comparison *estimates* a
difference. Reserve stronger verbs for conclusions the design establishes.
Use “we” for a consequential research choice and name the measured object when
reporting its properties.

Let a conjunction open a sentence or paragraph when it carries the argument
forward. For example, “Because a story can name several issuers, the number of
article–issuer links can exceed the number of distinct articles.” The reader
can recover the conclusion from the premise. When a paragraph ends with a
change in coverage, begin the next with the implication for the sample facing
the estimator. If that implication requires another assumption, state it at the
join. A short sentence can give a definition or finding room to register.

During review, read each difficult transition from the perspective of a finance
reader encountering the extraction method and an NLP reader encountering the
market convention. Identify what each must already know. Expand the missing
premise, keeping the technical detail, sample restriction and uncertainty that
give the result its meaning. Judge cadence by reading the passage in context.
Sentence lengths and conjunction frequencies are editorial choices.

## Carry a question forward

Follow the order in which a researcher can establish a result. For example,
the publisher filter identifies a population of captured stories. Linking those
stories to dated issuers lets the reader count coverage by firm. Requiring the
firm to be the subject of a story changes that count again. Each operation
answers the previous question and gives the reader a reason to ask the next.
Use chronological language for dated events and describe analytical dependencies
in terms of the evidence each step supplies.

A constructive passage can report an adverse result directly. For example,
“Recovering publication times increased the number of evaluated days with both
portfolio legs from 42 to 110. Mean drift remained negative on the expanded
sample.” The first sentence establishes what the timing work achieved. The
second answers the question that achievement makes possible. Report uncertainty
where the reader interprets the estimate. Carry the implication into the next
paragraph only when the next question depends on it.
